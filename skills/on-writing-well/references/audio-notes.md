# Notes from the author's audio teaching

Source: William Zinsser, [*On Writing Well Audio Collection*](https://www.audible.ca/pd/On-Writing-Well-Audio-Collection-Audiobook/B071RX5P1R),
read by the author. This is an abridged collection. The general nonfiction
program ends around 00:58:05; *How to Write a Memoir* begins at 00:58:07 and
continues through the rest of the approximately 2:23:19 recording.

These original, paraphrased notes were prepared from a private transcription
of a purchased copy on October 25, 2026. The recording and transcription are
not included or required to use this skill. Times are approximate and do not
establish coverage of the complete printed book. Do not treat uncertain ASR
spellings, dates, or quotations as verified text.

## Practices supported by the recording

- **01:19–04:10; 26:23–27:39 — Craft and revision.** Difficulty is normal.
  Both drafting a whole piece before revising and revising sentence by
  sentence can work. Choose a method that helps this writer develop the work.
- **04:34–05:26 — Test the actual meaning.** A sentence can convey something
  different from the author's intention. Check its plausible reading rather
  than grammatical correctness alone.
- **07:54–12:45 — Examine what each part contributes.** Remove clutter
  while preserving substance and personality. The cut-by-half exercise is
  not a mandatory compression target for every piece.
- **13:05–17:30 — Find the real lead.** An opening may be buried beneath
  preliminary paragraphs. Look for specificity, involvement, and a relaxed
  voice; test beginning there.
- **19:43–23:11 — Retain the writer's interests and judgments.** Help the
  reader understand without making universal approval the goal.
- **30:30–42:32 — Make action imaginable.** Concrete subjects and active
  verbs can clarify abstract noun clusters. Our adaptation keeps useful
  specialist terms and treats passive voice according to context.
- **45:42–49:59 — Read and listen.** Learn from admired writers; read drafts
  aloud. Consider the reader's state of knowledge at each sentence boundary
  and supply a bridge when time, place, mood, or direction changes.
- **50:15–55:01 — Let evidence carry meaning.** Avoid repeatedly prescribing
  a reaction that the facts or story already support. Keep explanations
  needed for a real factual or logical connection.
- **55:07–57:26 — Obtain real voices.** Interviews supply knowledge and
  distinctive speech. Listening and arrangement matter more than a particular
  recording or note-taking medium.
- **01:02:25–01:08:11; 01:31:29–01:40:42 — Discover and bound the subject.**
  A memoir selects a window into a life. Drafting concrete episodes can help
  reveal its subject before the final structure is chosen.
- **01:22:40–01:26:40; 01:54:13–01:59:27 — Choose a narrative perspective.**
  Distinguish remembered experience from retrospective understanding. A
  narrator's perspective and central relationships can require a structural
  rewrite that sentence polishing will not solve.

## Adaptation boundaries

The recording contains strong personal preferences about grammar, drafting
tools, and memoir attitudes. Apply the underlying craft with judgment. Do
not impose a single work ritual, ban a useful technical term, invent private
experience, or require an author to adopt a particular emotional conclusion.
Exercises, checklists, and technical-document boundaries in this skill are
our implementation choices.
