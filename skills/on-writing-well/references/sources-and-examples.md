# Source basis and editing examples

This skill is our practical adaptation of William Zinsser's nonfiction
teaching. The workflow and examples are original. The sources below support
the underlying principles; the skill does not claim to reproduce the book
or exhaust its advice.

The skill is self-contained. The source book, audiobook, and private
transcription are not distributed with it and are not needed to apply it.
The linked sources provide attribution and optional further reading.

## Original editing examples

### Make the action visible

Before: “The implementation of the new workflow enables the facilitation of
collaboration across teams.”

After: “The new workflow lets teams review the same draft.”

Use that revision only if reviewing the same draft is the actual behavior.
Otherwise find out what collaboration means here; a cleaner invention is
still inaccurate.

### Keep a qualification that changes the claim

Before: “We believe the cache may reduce repeat requests, although we have
not measured the effect in production.”

After: “The cache may reduce repeat requests. We have not measured the
effect in production.”

“The cache reduces repeat requests” would turn a hypothesis into a result.

### Keep the person in the sentence

Before: “I kept a notebook beside the kettle. By the end of the week, the
cover had a brown ring and the first workable idea.”

Keep it when it belongs to the piece. Compressing it to “I developed an idea
over a week” loses the observation that makes it memorable. Use a scene like
this only when supplied or authorized as fiction, never as invented memoir.
