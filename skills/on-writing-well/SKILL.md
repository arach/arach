# On Writing Well

Help the writer say something worth reading in their own voice. Apply
Zinsser's nonfiction craft through the practical workflow below. The aim is
prose that communicates fully and feels alive.

For the source basis and original editing examples, read
[sources and examples](references/sources-and-examples.md) when useful.
For timestamped practices from the author's recording, read
[audio notes](references/audio-notes.md) when useful.

## Establish the piece

Infer the occasion, reader, purpose, and desired voice from the request and
draft. Ask only when a missing answer would change the piece materially.
Find its central thought and choose a scope it can develop properly.
For exploratory personal writing, a few concrete episodes can help reveal
the subject before a finished outline is possible. Use that discovery to
choose the eventual shape.

When editing, preserve the author's position, facts, qualifications, useful
examples, and distinctive turns of phrase. If the thought is unclear, resolve
that before polishing sentences. Mark an unresolved factual gap instead of
inventing an answer.

## Write with substance and personality

- **Clarity:** make the thought understandable to someone encountering it for
  the first time. Name actors and actions when they matter. Explain the link
  between ideas instead of making the reader supply it.
- **Simplicity:** prefer familiar, exact language. Turn abstract noun clusters
  into people or things doing something. Keep a necessary specialist term and
  explain it at the reader's level.
- **Brevity:** remove language that adds no meaning, movement, or pleasure.
  Cut ceremonial openings, repetition, empty intensifiers, and needless
  qualifications. Preserve uncertainty that affects the claim.
- **Humanity:** keep the writer's curiosity, judgment, warmth, and humor where
  they belong. Use first person when it fits the author and occasion. Never
  supply personal memories or feelings the author has not provided.

Use active verbs when they reveal the action. Keep passive voice when the
actor is unknown, unimportant, or less useful than the thing affected.
Choose a word for its meaning, not its apparent sophistication or its origin.

## Give the piece a shape

Choose a consistent point of view, time frame, and register. Change them
deliberately when the subject requires it. Give each paragraph a clear job
and let its last thought prepare the reader for what comes next.

Open with the material that gives this piece a reason to exist: a concrete
fact, observation, scene, problem, or claim. A practical email can start with
the decision; an essay can earn its argument through a scene. Avoid a broad
preface that could introduce any subject.
When revising a lead, test whether preliminary paragraphs can go so the piece
begins where the writer becomes specific and involved.

Develop the thought with evidence and telling details. End when the piece has
delivered what it promised. Choose a final detail, implication, or action
that belongs to this piece. Add a recap only when it helps the reader.
Let facts and stories support an insight without repeatedly prescribing the
reader's reaction. Retain explanations needed for a factual or logical link.

## Revise for the reader

First revise substance and order: repair a missing explanation, move a buried
point, remove a digression, or replace a generality with an available detail.
Then revise sentences for exactness, economy, and rhythm. Notice repeated
openings, ambiguous references, awkward joins, and prose that sounds stiff.
Vary sentence length where it serves the thought.
Read aloud when useful. At each transition, consider what the reader knows
and whether a change of time, place, or direction needs a clearer bridge.

Keep useful development and the writer's character. A longer clear sentence
can serve the piece better than a sequence of clipped ones. A specific
example can deserve more space than a compressed abstraction.

For specifications, procedures, and technical contracts, preserve the
applicable house standard, exact terminology, requirement strength, and
failure conditions. Apply this craft to their surrounding explanation.

Return the requested draft or revision directly. Add a short editorial note
only when requested or needed to explain a consequential change or unresolved
question. Do not append an automatic score, checklist, or explanation of the
skill to ordinary writing output.
