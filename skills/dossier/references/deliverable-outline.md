# Dossier outline and evidence tables

Adapt the structure to the assignment. Omit irrelevant sections and acknowledge
unresolved requested facts. Do not manufacture content to fill the outline.

## Main report

1. **Identity and assessment.** Canonical business, domain, legal name, research
   cutoff, what it sells, and the findings that most affect the reader's decision.
2. **Founders and origin.** Relevant professional histories, prior collaboration,
   original insight, and contemporaneous versus retrospective evidence.
3. **Company chronology.** Incorporation/registration, prototype, early customer,
   product launch and financing milestones with date type and source.
4. **First customers and design partners.** Explicit identity finding, relationship
   status, initial workflow, paid status, product influence and limits.
5. **Narrative evolution.** A dated comparison of who the product was for, what it
   promised, how it was described and what proof supported it. Interpret change
   and continuity without assuming a pivot.
6. **Product and application.** User, buyer, workflows, access model and observed
   application behavior. Identify demonstrations and untested capabilities.
7. **Technology and delivery.** Public architecture, integrations, deployment,
   implementation services and documentation. Separate hiring goals from shipped work.
8. **Traction and economics.** Named customers, measured outcomes, funding,
   commercial model and operating effort, with attribution and missing denominators.
9. **Trust and operating constraints.** Material security, contractual or
   reliability claims, their evidence and access gaps.
10. **Position and implications.** Alternatives and what the evidence suggests
    for the user's question. Label analysis and avoid unsupported market rankings.
11. **Discrepancies and next evidence.** Conflicts, unknowns, and the specific
    documents or demonstrations that would resolve them.
12. **Coverage and artifacts.** Source register, archive receipts and inspected
    surfaces. State what the research did and did not establish.

## Durable package

Preserve the user's requested format. Otherwise a practical package is an
editable report, a readable rendered report when useful, and `sources.csv`.
Add `timeline.csv` or a structured history-evidence file for substantial historical
work. Add a site inventory and screenshot index only when that inspection occurred.
Do not require a fixed page, word, source or slide count.

Source entries should identify the underlying publisher and account. Retain
archive URLs and capture times, not just links to today's page. Optional response
hashes identify the retrieved bytes; they do not authenticate the underlying claim.
Store original analysis and bounded evidence extracts, not a mirror of copyrighted
source material. Keep private research out of public source repositories unless
the user has authorized publication of that material.

For an update, reconcile the new evidence into the report and registers, and
refresh derived counts and affected presentation pages. Keep prior access limits
visible until a new observation actually resolves them. Finish with artifact links,
the material conclusion and the remaining evidence gap, rather than a transcript
of the research steps.
